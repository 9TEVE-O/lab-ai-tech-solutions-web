## 2. Counting rule

A sector is included only where the reviewed material contains at least one substantive signal:

- direct operational, client-related or professional evidence;
- a reusable system, package, standard, prototype or product;
- a bounded research programme or developed analytical framework; or
- a documented idea with enough definition to identify the sector.

Generic prompt examples, incidental mentions, copied reference material and unsupported claims do not independently qualify a sector.

Closely related subindustries are consolidated to avoid count inflation.

---

## 3. State model

Each sector receives two separate states.

### Evidence state

| State | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `direct evidence` | Operational, client-related, case-study or professional material exists. This does not automatically prove a paid LAB engagement. |
| `built capability` | LAB produced a reusable system, package, prototype, method, standard or product relevant to the sector. |
| `research` | LAB completed substantive research, analysis or framework development, but not sector delivery proof. |
| `idea only` | A documented concept exists without sufficient build, research or delivery evidence. |

### Lifecycle state

| State | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `active` | Currently relevant to LAB’s operating direction, research, product work or opportunity development. |
| `completed` | A bounded capability, project or artefact was completed and may be reused as proof of capability or reference. |
| `parked` | Deliberately held outside active execution. Reactivation requires an explicit decision. |
| `archived` | Retained as historical, superseded or legacy reference. It is not current authority. |

**Control rule:** Evidence state and lifecycle state must not be collapsed. A sector can have strong direct evidence while no longer being active.

## 5. Public positioning domains

### Domain 1 — AI and software systems

**Included sectors:** 1, 12, 20  
**Core opportunity:** Controlled AI systems, agentic application architecture, AI-assisted development, workflow products and technical governance.  
**Current strength:** Strongest reusable capability base.  
**Main risk:** Overstating production readiness, security assurance, buyer validation or deployment history.

### Domain 2 — Document-heavy and regulated workflows

**Included sectors:** 8, 9, 13  
**Core opportunity:** Evidence-sensitive intake, document-risk review, classification, redaction, traceability, review gates and human decision records.  
**Current strength:** Clear method and completed sensitive-workflow artefacts.  
**Main risk:** Implying legal, clinical, regulatory or compliance authority.

### Domain 3 — Music, arts and creative operations

**Included sectors:** 2, 3, 6  
**Core opportunity:** Music-business systems, creative operations, knowledge bases, grants, publishing and structured AI-assisted workflows.  
**Current strength:** Direct domain background and differentiated founder context.  
**Main risk:** Reopening superseded offer structures or allowing the creative lane to fragment the current AI Work Control position.

### Domain 4 — Events, logistics and public-sector operations

**Included sectors:** 4, 7, 10, 11, 15  
**Core opportunity:** Operational documentation, contractor and staff systems, safety workflows, logistics, public-sector evidence handling and supply-chain assessment.  
**Current strength:** Strongest concentration of direct real-world operational evidence.  
**Main risk:** Treating prior professional work as current LAB client proof or as technical certification.

### Domain 5 — Education, research and knowledge systems

**Included sectors:** 5, 14, 16, 17, 18, 19  
**Core opportunity:** Research operating systems, teaching assets, knowledge pipelines, public-interest research and interactive learning patterns.  
**Current strength:** High research and reusable-method density.  
**Main risk:** Confusing internally developed frameworks with peer review, field validation, institutional adoption or public authority.
